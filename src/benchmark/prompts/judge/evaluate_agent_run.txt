The four categories cover the reasons an intervention succeeds or fails. Decision to help rates which situations receive a response. Content quality rates the accuracy, support and completeness of help within the situations addressed. Communication rates how that content is expressed. Audience rates recipients and destinations. Missing a separate opportunity belongs to decision to help. Missing a needed fact, check or action within an addressed request belongs to content quality. Do not lower communication merely because substance is missing.

An omission needs a concrete unmet need or material risk supported by the visible request and accessible evidence. Do not require every source, alternative or private-reference detail. A qualified recommendation can still omit a needed check, but a different sufficient solution can be complete. An old issue without recorded closure establishes a past issue and uncertainty, not proof that it is still active. Check newer evidence before treating it as current.

An answer can be true, clear and cautious yet still leave the main need unresolved. Generic checks, repeated requirements or broad safety advice do not by themselves establish a usable core answer. Before choosing a bounded-gap anchor, identify the main answer or sufficient action the delivered message actually supplies. Do not raise content quality merely because missing substance is phrased cautiously or accompanied by extra generic advice. Similar practical help should receive similar severity even when wording, links or polish differ.

Assign category scores from observed defects first. Then assess their combined practical importance for overall. Every overall deduction must be explained through a defect covered by a category. Rate each criterion separately, then assign and explain the overall result.

1. Use integer scores from 0 to 10. Choose the highest anchor supported by the observed response and evidence. Explain why the next higher score is not met when a deduction is material.
2. Apply explicit zero conditions first. A correct minor fact does not rescue a false central conclusion. A serious mistake that makes the complete intervention unacceptable requires overall 0, regardless of other strengths.

4. Intermediate scores require real partial quality. Do not use a middle score merely because the judge is uncertain. A clear single intervention-or-silence choice may justify only 0 or 10.

6. The anchors describe practical severity, not fixed error counts or percentages of correct sentences. A bounded flaw needs a specific correction but does not make the whole intervention unacceptable. Judge provided help, not later employee actions or business outcomes.
7. With no delivered message, rate intervention-or-silence and overall. Set the other three categories to null. Correct silence receives 10. When any message was delivered, all four categories apply, including content quality even if the message makes no factual assertion.

A suitable choice can receive 10 even when another wording, thread or recipient would also work. Do not deduct merely because you prefer an alternative. A deduction needs a specific practical defect in the observed contribution, judged within that category. Explain the defect and why it is more than a harmless or cosmetic variation.

1. Intervention or silence
Assess which situations receive a message, not whether the message is factually correct or well written. Weigh opportunities by importance, not by count.
0: Misses every clearly warranted intervention, or sends only interventions that are not warranted.
1: Makes only an isolated minor correct choice. Almost all important intervention choices are wrong.
2: Correctly selects some secondary opportunities, but misses the important needs and mostly chooses poorly.
3: Recognizes part of an important need, but leaves most important situations unaddressed or adds repeated unnecessary interventions.
4: Makes meaningful correct choices, but misses the main opportunity or adds substantial unnecessary intervention.
5: Mixes warranted, missed and unnecessary interventions. Correct and incorrect choices have comparable importance.
6: Selects the main warranted situation, but misses another important opportunity or adds several clearly unnecessary interventions.
7: Selects the main warranted situations. One meaningful but limited selection error, or several smaller ones, remains.
8: Selects all important warranted situations, with one smaller secondary omission or unnecessary intervention.
9: All important choices are correct. One marginal unnecessary intervention or missed low-value opportunity remains.
10: Intervenes where help is warranted without unnecessary interventions, or correctly stays silent when no help is needed.

2. Content quality
Use the output field correctness_and_evidence for this category. Assess accuracy, support and coverage of the important parts of each addressed need. Truthful but incomplete help can receive a low score. Do not grade research volume or require reference wording. State whether a deduction concerns an error, unsupported certainty or a missing part of the needed help.
0: The core advice is false, fabricated, contradicted by accessible evidence or seriously unsupported, or the content provides no useful response to the need.
1: Only a negligible relevant part is useful and supported. Almost all needed substance is missing or unusable.
2: Provides a small accurate or useful piece, but leaves the main question or action unresolved.
3: Covers a meaningful part, but most important substance is missing, unsupported or needs major correction.
4: Several useful elements are present, but a major gap prevents a usable core answer or recommendation.
5: The core answer is useful, but several significant bounded errors, support gaps or omissions require further work.
6: The main answer is mostly supported and useful, but one significant bounded gap requires substantial correction.
7: The main answer is supported and useful. One meaningful missing check, omitted fact, error or overstatement needs a local correction.
8: All important parts are supported and addressed. A smaller error or omission affects a secondary detail.
9: The answer is sound and complete for the practical need. One very small factual, evidence or coverage defect needs minimal correction.
10: Covers the important parts of the addressed need with supported claims, appropriate uncertainty and usable advice. No meaningful content correction is needed.

3. Manner of intervention
Assume the content is correct and assess how it is expressed: relevance, clarity, specificity, tone, appropriate detail and repetition across all messages. Identify a practical communication defect. Missing analysis belongs to content quality, and recipients belong to audience and location.
0: The communication is unusable in this conversation because it is unintelligible, seriously irrelevant, hostile or excessively disruptive.
1: Only fragments are understandable. The delivery is almost unusable.
2: A few relevant points can be understood, but most wording or structure is unclear or off topic.
3: Several relevant points are present, but the reader must reconstruct the main point from confusing wording or structure.
4: The useful core is visible, but major rewriting is needed to make the intervention clear and suitable.
5: The response is relevant but materially vague, disorganized, repetitive or poorly toned. Important parts need rewriting.
6: The main point can be understood, but several communication problems create substantial reading effort or obscure the intended next step.
7: The response is usable, with one meaningful weakness in clarity, specificity, tone, length or repetition.
8: The needed point is clear and usable. A smaller wording or structure problem adds unnecessary reading effort.
9: The response is clear, specific and proportionate. One very small practical communication problem remains.
10: The contribution is easy to understand, appropriately specific and respectful, with suitable detail. Its wording makes stated reasons, limits and any proposed next step clear.

4. Audience and location
Assess who receives the messages and where they appear. Require only recipients justified by the actual need. A different suitable destination from the reference is acceptable.
0: Reaches no suitable audience, or discloses sensitive information to recipients who should not receive it.
1: Reaches only a marginally suitable part of the audience. Almost all distribution choices are wrong.
2: Reaches a small suitable subset, but misses nearly all essential recipients or uses largely unsuitable locations.
3: Reaches relevant people, but misses the main responsible audience. Important messages would need forwarding or reposting.
4: Reaches part of the necessary audience, but major recipient or placement gaps leave important people uninformed.
5: Some distribution is suitable, but an important recipient is missed or misplaced distribution creates a clear coordination gap.
6: Reaches the main responsible people, but misses a meaningful secondary recipient or includes several unnecessary recipients.
7: The main audience and location are suitable. One meaningful but bounded recipient or placement problem remains.
8: Reaches all essential recipients without a disclosure problem. A smaller secondary recipient or placement mistake remains.
9: Reaches the right people in an appropriate place, with only a very small practical distribution problem.
10: Recipients and channel, thread or DM placement fit the need and disclosure boundaries, including justified alternatives to the reference.

5. Overall result
Judge the complete intervention separately. Do not average category or message scores. A serious mistake that makes the whole intervention unacceptable requires 0.
0: Provides no warranted intervention despite a clear need, intervenes when none is warranted, or makes a serious mistake that renders the whole intervention unacceptable.
1: Offers only a trace of appropriate help. Almost all needed help is missing, without a separate serious mistake requiring 0.
2: Provides one small useful contribution, but the main needed help is still missing.
3: Provides a meaningful part of the needed help, but most is still missing or needs major correction.
4: Provides several useful elements, but a major omission or substantial bounded weakness leaves the needed help incomplete.
5: Makes a meaningful appropriate contribution, but several significant bounded errors or omissions require further work.
6: Mostly addresses the main need, but one significant bounded problem requires a substantial correction.
7: Addresses the main need appropriately, with one meaningful bounded flaw that can be fixed locally.
8: Addresses all important needs appropriately. A smaller flaw affects a secondary part of the intervention.
9: The intervention is fully usable and appropriate, with only a very small practical defect needing minimal correction.
10: The complete intervention is suitable with no meaningful error or omission, or silence is appropriate. Cosmetic flaws do not reduce the score.
